An electric water heater has no burner, no flame, and no vent, so it is tempting to think nothing much can go wrong. In practice, the parts that do the work (two heating elements, two thermostats, and a high-limit safety switch) fail in predictable ways. The upper thermostat heats the top third of the tank first, then switches power to the lower element. Lose the upper element or thermostat and the lower element never gets power, so the whole tank goes cold. If the lower element burns out, the top of the tank still heats, so you get a short burst of hot water that runs out fast. For Reno, NV homeowners, knowing which symptom you have is the first step toward the right fix.

Reno Water Heater tests electric heaters with a meter, not a hunch. With power confirmed off, our technicians measure element resistance and look for a short to the tank, a common failure that can keep tripping the high-limit. Then, following careful live-testing procedure, we verify voltage at the thermostats and watch how power moves from the upper side to the lower side. We look for scorched wires, melted insulation, and loose terminal screws, since a loose connection can heat up enough to damage the thermostat and the wiring around it. If the reset button has tripped, we find out why before resetting it, because a high-limit that trips is doing its job.

Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • No hot water at all from any faucet
  • Browned wiring or a burnt odor from behind the access cover
  • Rusty water, leaks around an element, or water at the base
  • The high-limit reset popping again after you push it
  • Tap water much hotter than the thermostat setting
  • Crackling noises as the heater runs, often from sediment
  • A breaker that trips when the heater calls for heat
  • A shower that turns lukewarm much sooner than it used to

Act Before It Gets Worse

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. A quick call to (775) 314-3959 gets a technician out to check it before it becomes an emergency.

Why Professional Electric Water Heater Services Matters

Two things make electric water heater repair trickier than it looks: the power involved and the overlap between symptoms. Every element and thermostat connects to 240-volt terminals, and those terminals stay live until the breaker is off and the circuit is tested dead. On the diagnostic side, a heater that will not heat might have a failed element, a bad thermostat, a tripped reset, or a wiring problem, and a reset that keeps tripping is a warning, not an annoyance. Guessing gets expensive fast.

Professional service gets you the right fix the first time and a safe heater afterward. Rather than guess, we meter each element and thermostat, check for shorts to the tank, and inspect every wire and terminal for heat damage. Repairs are done with the tank drained and refilled correctly, and we verify amp draw once power is back on. When replacement makes more sense, we confirm the breaker size and wire gauge match the new heater and install the expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps your code requires. You end up with hot water you can count on.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year
  • Keep the thermostats set around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • If the reset button trips, have it checked rather than pressing it over and over
  • Make sure water flows steadily from a hot tap before the heater's power goes back on
  • Flush the tank regularly to keep minerals from piling up around the lower element
  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
  • In hard water areas, a softener or low-watt-density elements can extend element life
