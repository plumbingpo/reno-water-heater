If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Reno, NV, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

If you have a tankless unit, the routine changes but the goal is the same. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually causing shutdowns. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the water filter, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. You get a straightforward report after every visit, noting what is healthy and what needs attention. Call (775) 314-3959 to book maintenance for your Reno water heater.

Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures

Why Professional Water Heater Maintenance Matters

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which affect your household's air.

We come prepared for what an older heater throws at us: replacement drain valves, anode rods sized for your tank, flexible anodes for tight clearances, T&P valves, and meters for testing elements and thermostats. For tankless systems, we bring the pump and descaling setup. Just as important, we know what to look for, from early corrosion at the fittings to a thermocouple starting to weaken, so you can plan a repair before it becomes a failure. The goal is simple: a heater that lasts longer, runs more efficiently, and does not surprise you with a leak.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
