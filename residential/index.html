No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Reno Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing on every job, covering everything from a failed heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Watch for discolored or rusty hot water, popping noises when the burner fires, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually a thick layer of sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a failing dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign usually costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is always less expensive and less stressful than an emergency swap with water on the floor. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions affect tank life, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Reno Homes

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we drain and flush the tank, measure the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, look over the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all matched to the heater in your home.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. An older tank gets a closer look than a brand-new heater, a tankless unit on hard water calls for regular descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a large family cycles the tank far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every tune-up helps keep the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

The savings show up quickly. A flushed tank heats water more efficiently, a replaced anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Compared with the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is money well spent.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can sustain at your incoming water temperature. We don't simply copy the size of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up after two teenagers start showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical service, and the footprint, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes in Reno run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Reno Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also verify the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
