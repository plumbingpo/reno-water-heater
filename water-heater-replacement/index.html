How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Age is the first clue. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. Then look at the symptoms. A leak from the tank itself, rusty water from the hot side only, and loud sediment noise all point to a tank near the end. A thermocouple, an element, or a relief valve can be swapped on almost any heater; a rusted-out tank cannot, and homeowners in Reno, NV usually find that out the hard way, with a wet floor.

Replacing a water heater is a chance to get the next one right. Reno Water Heater sizes the new unit to your household, not just to the label on the old one. With a tank, the number that matters is the first-hour rating, which measures the hot water delivered in a busy hour, more than the gallon count. For tankless units, it means sizing to flow rate in gallons per minute and to the temperature rise your incoming water needs in winter. We walk you through the real trade-offs between gas and electric, tank and tankless, and standard and heat pump models, in plain language and without pressure, and we confirm that your gas line, venting, and electrical service can support the choice before you commit.

Installation day runs on a checklist: old tank drained and removed, fresh connectors and a working shutoff put in, a sediment trap added and the gas line leak-tested, venting sized and pitched to the manufacturer's spec, and the relief valve piped to a safe discharge point. If local code requires a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps, they go in, and the permit is pulled whenever one is needed. We finish by filling and purging the tank, checking draft and carbon monoxide on gas models, setting the thermostat near 120 degrees, and showing you around the new heater. When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

Start with the venting. A gas water heater's exhaust contains carbon monoxide, and a vent that is undersized, poorly pitched, or loosely connected can let it spill back into your home. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and there is a lot to get right. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. A move to tankless or a heat pump brings gas line sizing, new vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines into the job.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
