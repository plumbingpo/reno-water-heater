Keeping Your Lemmon Valley Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Most water heater failures in Lemmon Valley give warning signs before they happen. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon.
